Executive summary

Klårt sits in a quadrant nobody else occupies, a native, local-first editor whose AI only critiques. That position is real, evidence-backed and cheap to serve, but today it is a feature with no distribution. The business case turns on two things the current build does not yet have: a reason to return on day 30, and a way to reach writers where their documents already live.

  1. The whitespace is real but thin. No competitor combines Klårt's three traits, yet the critique itself is a prompt. Grammarly, Lex, ProWritingAid, Claude's learning style and ChatGPT Study Mode all critique on request3,5,7,37,38. Defensibility has to come from the workflow (read on section completion, anchored notes, respond loop), from memory (the planned writer profile) and from rubric taste, not from the feature.
  2. The "coaching, not ghostwriting" thesis is scientifically defensible. A field RCT with about 1,000 students found unrestricted GPT lowered unassisted exam scores while a guard-railed tutor that withheld answers removed the harm47. Knowledge workers who trust GenAI more report less critical thinking45, heavier AI use correlates with cognitive offloading78, and AI-assisted essays converge in structure79. This is a marketing asset in education and advisory work, where AI generation is restricted but AI feedback is explicitly permitted33,34.
  3. Pricing should follow the BYO-key Mac cohort, not the SaaS writing tools. Zero marginal cost and a privacy audience point to a one-time license with twelve months of updates, the model that produced TypingMind's first $1M in twenty months and MacWhisper's roughly $2M cumulative62,65. Recommendation: free offline checks, €59 one-time, €29 yearly renewal, an optional €8 per month hosted-model tier for people who will never install Ollama, education at half price, team seats at €99 per year.
  4. Beachhead: individual advisors and doctoral writers in the EU, privacy first. Consultants already know MECE and the pyramid, buy their own tools, and handle client data they cannot paste into ChatGPT. BCG's internal Deckster ships a "review this" button precisely because juniors want critique against senior standards41. Doctoral writers get scarce supervisor feedback and are covered by policies that allow feedback tools33.
  5. The biggest risk is not competition, it is the note silo. Klårt asks writers to move their notes into its own store. Every comparable that grew without marketing (Obsidian, iA Writer, Typora) works on plain files the user already owns17,11,52. Opening any folder of markdown is the single highest-leverage change in the backlog.
  6. Three structural issues need a decision before launch. The MIT license lets anyone ship the paid product. The default model (llama3.2, 3B parameters) cannot deliver the critique quality the pitch promises, so the first impression is the weakest one. Apple's on-device Foundation Models and free Private Cloud Compute tier on macOS 26/27 remove both setup friction and model cost, but only for an App Store build72,73.
  7. Recommendation. Do not choose between "indie one-time app" and the five alternatives in section 8 yet. Run the 90-day validation in section 7 with two segments, measure sections read per week, respond rate and day-30 retention, and let those numbers pick the path. The indie base case is a plausible €50–300k per year business; only the vertical and platform paths have a ceiling above that.

2 · Market context

Everyone sells generation; the few who sell critique sell it as a cloud upload

Two axes separate the field: how much the AI writes for you, and where the model runs. Klårt is alone in the "never writes, runs locally" corner. The corner is small, but everything adjacent to it is either free (iA Writer's restraint, Argdown, Hemingway classic) or a per-manuscript web service.

2.3 Demand and platform signals

Firms are building critique internally

BCG's Deckster, used weekly by about 40% of associates, has a "review this" button that grades a junior's slide against senior best practice41. McKinsey's Lilli serves over 75% of 43,000 staff and includes a tone-of-voice checker40. Amazon's six-page memo culture is being eroded by internal AI drafting tools, and employees report lost rigour42. Demand for AI review of structured argument exists; it is currently met inside firms, not by products.

Education permits feedback, restricts generation

Liverpool lists "getting feedback on work prior to submission" as acceptable use; King's, Oxford and Toronto follow the same shape, with disclosure33,34. Grammarly for Education claims 3,000+ institutions39. Anthropic and OpenAI shipped Socratic modes in 2025 and are signing campuses at roughly $24 per seat per year36,35. A tool that structurally cannot write is easier to approve than one that promises not to.

Apple leaves the critique gap open

Writing Tools on macOS 15–27 proofread, rewrite, summarise and compose in any text field, free, and offer no editorial feedback mode71. The Foundation Models framework gives apps a free on-device 3B model; macOS 27 adds a LanguageModel protocol, a 32K-context Private Cloud Compute model at no API cost for Small Business Program apps under 2M downloads, and swappable Anthropic and Google packages72,73. Setup friction and model cost, Klårt's two adoption taxes, largely disappear for an App Store build.

Local AI is now a mainstream developer habit

Ollama reports 8.9M monthly active developers and raised $65M in July 2026; LM Studio is free for commercial use; Jan has over 5M downloads67,68. The pool of people who already run a model locally is large, technical, privacy-motivated and Mac-heavy. It is Klårt's cheapest first audience and its least representative one.

Beachhead A

The independent advisor

Senior associate to partner, or solo consultant, DACH and EU. Writes recommendation memos and proposal narratives weekly, was trained on MECE and the pyramid, and cannot paste client material into a public chatbot. Buys their own tools when the firm's are slow.

Job to be done: "Tell me where a partner will tear this apart before the partner does."
Buying trigger: a review that hurt. Channel: LinkedIn, consulting newsletters, StrategyU-type communities that already sell pyramid-principle prompt packs43.

Beachhead B

The doctoral writer

Years two to four of a PhD in a text-heavy field. Gets supervisor feedback every few months, is explicitly allowed to use feedback tools but not generation, and already writes in markdown or would for a good editor. Price sensitive, time rich, and talks to peers.

Job to be done: "Give me reviewer two before reviewer two." Buying trigger: a chapter due to the supervisor. Channel: university writing centres, PhD subreddits and Discords, Obsidian and Zotero communities; education pricing.

Second wave

The memo-culture operator

Founder, PM or staff engineer whose company runs on written narratives. Highest Mac and markdown fit of any segment and comfortable with local models. Privacy matters less than speed, so the hosted-model tier is for them.

Job to be done: "Make my six-pager survive the meeting." Buying trigger: a strategy doc or RFC that got shredded. Channel: Hacker News, Product Hunt, engineering-management newsletters.

4.3 Channel and licence decisions

Sell direct and on the App Store, in that order

Direct via Paddle or Lemon Squeezy keeps 90%+ and allows the one-time-plus-renewal model cleanly. The App Store takes 15% under the Small Business Program76 but is the gate to Apple's free cloud model tier and to TestFlight, which the repo already targets73,1. iA Writer sells both ways12. Setapp is a third channel later: 70% of a subscriber's fee is pooled by app usage, so a daily-use writing tool earns disproportionately77.

Decide the licence before the first sale

MIT lets anyone rebuild and sell the binary. Three workable positions: keep MIT and sell convenience only (signed builds, hosted model, sync), like Jan and Obsidian's plugin economy; move the app to a source-available licence such as FSL or BUSL while keeping KlartKit's checks MIT; or close the app and open the rubric format. The recommendation is the middle path. It preserves the trust that a privacy product needs and still protects the paid surface.

5 · Klårt read by its own editor

The business case, put through the nine lenses

Each note follows Klårt's own shape: the words it is about, the observation, and what it costs the argument. Severity marks as in the app: ‼ the case fails without this, ! it is weakened.

◇
Gap ‼
"A thinking coach that lives in your notes"
Nothing in the product or the documents answers why a writer would move their notes into a new store to get the coach. The switching cost of a system of record is the highest in software, and the value on offer arrives only after the move.
Costs the argument its funnel: every comparable that grew organically works on files the user already has.
⊢
Assumption ‼
"Ollama (ollama pull llama3.2)"
The quick start assumes a 3B local model can find unstated warrants and the strongest unmet objection. It cannot, reliably. The pitch is written for a frontier-class critic; the default experience is a small one. First impressions are formed on the default.
Costs the argument its proof: users judge the thesis by the weakest read they get.
❝
Evidence !
"Feedback that the writer has to act on is what changes the writer"
Stated as fact and true in the literature for students, where corrective feedback beats reformulation47,48,49,75. No evidence yet that it holds for Klårt's adult writers, and the app collects verdicts but has no users producing them.
Without usage data the thesis stays borrowed; the learning log is the instrument and it is unplugged.
⇄
Counter !
"Coaching, not ghostwriting"
The strongest objection: most people want output, and the market prices it that way. Grammarly's $700M and Notion's AI-driven ARR are generation businesses4,22; critique-only tools top out around 10k to 80k users30,32. The case has not engaged this. The answer is that restraint is a niche, and niches are what indie tools win; the case should say so and size it.
Unanswered, it lets a reader dismiss the whole strategy as principled but small.
⧉
MECE !
"Academic researchers… Consultants and analysts… Technical writers"
These are three markets with different channels, price points and policies, listed as one audience. Technical writers in particular have no privacy premium and strong tooling already.
A launch aimed at all three reaches none; the scoring in section 3 orders them.
≡
Structure !
Commit history: 79 commits, 0 on distribution
The work is ordered inside-out: caret geometry, rail springs, vault encryption before a landing page or a price. The order hides the argument that this is a business rather than a craft object.
Every month on the surface is a month without the conversion data that would settle sections 7 and 8.
⇒
Warrant !
"Local-first & private"
The claim and the evidence (Keychain, AES-GCM, sensitive notes) are present. Why privacy should make someone pay is never said. For beachhead A it is a purchase condition; for the memo-culture operator it is indifferent.
Without the link, privacy reads as engineering pride rather than a segment's requirement.
◎
Clarity
"thinking coach", "editor", "notes app", "Teleprompter"
Four names for one product across README and PRD. A careful reader cannot say what category Klårt competes in, so cannot say what it replaces.
A product without a category has no comparison set in the buyer's head and defaults to "free chatbot".
?
Question
Day 30
What does a Klårt user do in week five that they could not do in week one? If the answer is "the same thing, with a critic that remembers nothing", retention will look like a utility's. The planned writer profile is the only feature that answers this, and it is not built.

7 · Questions that decide the business case

Twenty-two questions, four kill criteria, one 90-day plan

Ordered so that the cheapest questions come first. Most can be answered with a landing page, a TestFlight cohort of fifty and the verdict log that already exists.

Demand

  1. When a writer in beachhead A or B finishes a section today, what do they do to check it, and what does that cost them in time or embarrassment?
  2. How many sections per week does a target user actually finish? Below roughly five, a per-section critic is used too rarely to be remembered.
  3. Which lens produces notes users act on? Respond and Confirm rates by kind, from the log, over the first 30 days.
  4. What share of notes are rejected, and are rejections "wrong" or "true but unwelcome"? The second is the product working.
  5. Do users prefer critique on a finished section or on the whole document? The answer sets the roadmap in section 6.

Willingness to pay

  1. At €59 one-time, what share of a 1,000-visitor landing page pre-orders? Below 1% the indie path is a hobby.
  2. Does a hosted-model tier at €8 per month convert people who never installed Ollama? If not, the audience is only local-AI enthusiasts.
  3. Will an advisory firm pay €99 per seat for shared rubrics and a compliant endpoint, or does its own tool (Lilli, Deckster) make the question moot40,41?
  4. Will a writing centre or graduate school pay per seat for feedback-only AI, given it already pays Grammarly, Paperpal or OpenAI at $13–24 per seat35,39?

Differentiation and defensibility

  1. Blind test: Klårt's nine-lens read versus a one-paragraph prompt in Claude or ChatGPT on the same section. If users cannot tell them apart, the product is the workflow only.
  2. Does the writer profile change acceptance rates after 20 reads? This is the compounding claim; it must be measured, not asserted.
  3. Which local model is the minimum for a read users accept, and does it run on an 8 GB Mac? This sets the real addressable base.
  4. What happens to the rejection rate when the same rubric is run by Apple's on-device model versus a frontier model? Quality difference sets the hosted tier's value.

Distribution

  1. Will Obsidian users adopt Klårt as a second app on the same folder, or only as a plugin inside Obsidian?
  2. Which channel produces the first 100 paying users at under €20 acquisition cost: Hacker News, a PhD community, a consulting newsletter, or the Ollama ecosystem?
  3. Is App Store distribution worth 15% for TestFlight, Apple's free cloud model tier and discoverability, versus direct only76,73?
  4. Does publishing the nine-lens rubric as a standalone document get cited or shared? A rubric with reach is a marketing asset the app can ride.

Economics and structure

  1. What is the support cost per user of BYO-model setup? Provider and model debugging is the hidden cost of "any LLM".
  2. Which licence: stay MIT and sell convenience, or source-available for the app and MIT for KlartKit?
  3. Solo founder time: is 12 months of updates a promise that can be kept alongside a consulting practice?

Risk

  1. If Apple adds an editorial-feedback mode to Writing Tools in macOS 28, what remains? Rubric depth, anchoring, memory and the profile are the only durable answers.
  2. If Grammarly or Lex ship a "critique only" toggle, does Klårt's local-first stance still justify a separate app? Only for the segments that score privacy at 5 in section 3.

Kill criteria

  • Fewer than 5 finished sections per user per week after 30 days: usage too sparse for a per-section critic.
  • Respond plus Confirm below 30% of notes: the rubric is not landing, whatever the model.
  • Pre-order conversion below 1% on qualified traffic at €59: reprice, or leave the indie path.
  • Blind test shows no preference for Klårt's read over a plain chatbot prompt: the value is the editor, so price the editor and give the AI away.

90-day validation plan

Weeks 1–2
Landing page with positioning A, the published rubric, and a €59 pre-order button. Two ad-free channels per segment. Add opt-in, local-only usage counters to the app (sections read, verdicts), exported by the user, never sent automatically.
Weeks 3–6
TestFlight cohort of 50: 25 advisors, 25 doctoral writers. Ship open-folder mode and the Apple on-device provider first so the cohort starts without Ollama. Weekly export of verdict logs; ten interviews.
Weeks 7–10
Blind test (question 10) with 20 participants. Ship a minimal writer profile to half the cohort and compare acceptance rates.
Weeks 11–13
Price test: €59 versus €79 one-time, and the €8 hosted tier. Decide the path in section 8 against the kill criteria. Licence decision made and documented before the first charge.
8 · Five alternative approaches

9 · Recommendation

Validate the indie case for 90 days, but build it so alternatives 1 and 2 stay open

What to do now

  1. Rename the category: argument editor, positioning A. Publish the nine-lens rubric as a document with its own URL.
  2. Ship open-folder mode and the Apple on-device provider before any pricing test. Both remove reasons not to start.
  3. Build the writer profile as the retention feature; measure it against a control half of the cohort.
  4. Price at €59 one-time with 12 months of updates, €29 education, €8 hosted tier. Sell direct first, App Store second.
  5. Move the app to a source-available licence and keep KlartKit's local checks MIT, before the first sale.
  6. Run the 90-day plan with 25 advisors and 25 doctoral writers, and decide against the kill criteria.

What the decision looks like in December

If advisors convert and ask for shared rubrics, alternative 2 is the company: the founder's consulting network is the sales channel and the security model is already built.

If doctoral writers love it and cannot pay, alternative 1 is the route to reach, with alternative 3 as a later institutional layer.

If both segments use it weekly and pay €59, the indie base case is a sound business, and alternative 4 is the upgrade path once the free base is large.

If neither segment finishes five sections a week, the value is the editor rather than the critic. Sell a beautiful Mac markdown editor with a free editorial rail, and stop calling it a coach.
